# Two taps, not a hold

Amended by 0067 (the first tap also cuts the voice).

## Context

The room has one circle and no words, so the gesture that opens the microphone is the whole
interface. Most of the speech in the room is deliberately not addressed to the machine: the
team talks among themselves constantly.

## Considered Options

Press and hold while speaking. Rejected because it makes the team perform for the tablet
throughout the turn, and because a turn in this room can run for minutes.

## Decision

The first tap addresses the room and opens the microphone; the second says the team is
finished. The gesture is symmetrical and nothing depends on the finger staying down.

## Consequences

The team can put the tablet down mid-turn. Lifting a finger never ends a capture, so a held
press behaves as the first tap rather than as a separate gesture — it opens the microphone
and leaves it open.
